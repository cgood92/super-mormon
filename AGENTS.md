# Repository workflow

- Keep tests beside the module or feature they exercise (`*.test.ts`). Cross-world integration tests belong beside the world runtime; deployment tests belong beside their script. `npm test` discovers tests recursively under `src` and `scripts`.

- The brainstorming skill is optional in this repository. For concrete playtest feedback and clear feature or bugfix requests, proceed directly with investigation, implementation and verification; do not require a brainstorming session, a new specification or a separate design approval. Ask only for information that materially blocks the work.

# Super Mario 64 physics, controls, and camera parity

- Mimic _Super Mario 64_ in physics, kinematics, controls, character dynamics, and camera behavior wherever possible. Reference and copy exact formulas, constants, and state transitions from the Super Mario 64 decompilation (`n64decomp/sm64`: `mario.c`, `mario_actions_airborne.c`, `mario_actions_moving.c`, `mario_step.c`, `camera.c`) at every point possible.
- Jump mechanics must follow SM64 curves and progression: single jump ($v_y = 42\text{f} + 0.25\text{f} \cdot v_{\text{fwd}}$), double jump ($v_y = 52\text{f} + 0.25\text{f} \cdot v_{\text{fwd}}$), and triple jump ($v_y = 69\text{f}$, committing to full arc without short-hop cancellation). Apex hangtime, landing compression/crouch, airborne foot plantar flexion, and touchdown settling must match SM64 dynamics.
- Character motion and poses must mirror the iconic SM64 states: single jump punch & high knee, double jump hands-down leap, triple jump multi-phase somersault flip (launch $\to$ accelerated tight tuck $\to$ smooth unfold), long jump forward leap, wall slide/kick deflection, drift/skid turns, and swimming strokes.
- Camera behavior must replicate Lakitu / Mario camera dynamics: smooth trailing follow, snappy C-button orbits and 8-way directional stepping, distance presets, and floor/wall collision avoidance without clipping.

# Scenery acceptance rules

- Build foreground solid scenery with shared geometry-and-collision helpers in `src/game/worlds/shared/scenery.ts`. Use one dimension source for its visible surface and physical support. Register deliberate decoration/backdrops explicitly with a reason; do not turn all render meshes into enclosing AABBs.
- Trees must have solid trunks. Add climbables for trees intended to be climbed. Rocks and mountains need matching walkable terraces or carefully fitted physical surfaces, preserving story routes and clear paths around obstacles.
- New scenery must pass semantic collision validation and actual `stepPlayer` traversal checks (blocked walking, walking around, landing, and climbing as appropriate). Verify a normal player browser route, rather than only teleporting to story targets.
- Each walk surface has one owner. Avoid coplanar overlapping floor meshes; remove the duplicate or separate surfaces with real thickness. Check close and distant views and raycast representative surfaces to catch duplicate hits/z-fighting.
- Batch repeated rock tiers and plants where practical. Dispose instance buffers as well as geometry/materials when leaving a world. Check render workload after adding physical scenery so correctness does not quietly undermine mobile performance.
- Cinematic cameras must frame the player, stay clear of solid and opaque rendered geometry, and validate the entire entrance/watch/return path. Exercise shots from multiple approach angles and phone/desktop aspect ratios. Test recovery from an initially embedded camera and props that move into the shot; never assume a focus point is outside scenery.
- Close browser tabs opened for testing as soon as their checks are finished. Preserve the user's unrelated tabs. Hidden game pages must not keep rendering or accumulating elapsed simulation time.

# Game direction and story design

- Design adventures around enjoyable play related to the scripture: navigation, timing, discovery, environmental puzzles, carrying, herding, stealth or helping people. Repeated interaction-button presses and dialogue chains are not a core gameplay loop; short story scenes should support substantial player-controlled activity.
- Build generous places with multiple inhabitants, branching paths, accessible rooms or architecture, hidden discoveries and rewarding vistas. Include optional brief conversations and secrets that reward curiosity beyond the central story objective; optional finds must not gate sacred events.
- Physical foreground objects, including buildings, trunks, rocks, walls and substantial props, must block passage and provide matching support where walkable. Validate reachable routes around them with actual player movement.
- Target older children. Later chapters should demand more navigation, timing, stealth, climbing, or planning than early chapters. Difficulty may come from a large, rewarding world rather than faster enemies. Preserve fair retries and readable objectives.
- Research each story using the actual Book of Mormon chapters before designing it. Record primary-source links and distinguish scripture from invented dialogue, terrain, costumes, condensed chronology, and player roles. Do not present uncertain geography or reconstructions as established fact.
- Make every scene worth exploring beyond its main objective: discoverable architecture, side paths, rooms, vistas, and optional conversations. Use real named people only where their presence fits the source and chronology; imagined unnamed inhabitants are fine. Keep dialogue brief and substantive.
- Give named characters distinguishable faces, hair, silhouettes, clothing and equipment, using the approved sculpted cast. Communicate allegiance and temperament through costume, posture, expressions and behavior; never use skin color, ethnicity or body type as a marker of righteousness or wickedness.
- Important encounters can use short acted sequences with character motion and safely framed cameras, followed by player-controlled continuation. Keep sacred moments reverent. Avoid turning historical tragedies into changeable outcomes or graphic spectacle.
- The first six story paintings stay in the entrance gallery. Later paintings belong along side hallways, spreading to upper floors progressively. Never put level entrances, collectibles or gameplay objectives in the auditorium. Preserve free exploration of the Conference Center and its roof gardens.
- Undocumented scene-testing query parameters are developer conveniences. Never advertise them in player-facing menus, signs, help or onboarding, and never silently alter saved story progress for testing.
- Preserve smooth movement, device-specific control hints, mobile performance, lazy scene loading, collision, camera safety and resource disposal as scenes grow. Verify actual playable routes and retry/completion behavior, not only that a module constructs.
- Use Sol sub-agents for independent implementation work. Close only browser tabs opened for your own testing when finished. Commit and push verified batches to the personal GitHub repository; do not repeatedly monitor deployment pipelines.
- Character clothing checks must include every inner mesh (tunic, trousers and footwear), not only the shirt. Inspect actual rendered side and quarter views during sharp turns and reversals with velocity lag, as well as straight walking. Test both player and NPC geometry; preserve intentional front openings and cuffs while preventing legs or inner fabric from crossing covered coat panels.
- Character costumes use explicit visible surfaces: upper tunic/cuffs and a front insert beneath the outer coat, rather than a full buried skirt or tall hidden boot shafts. Do not reintroduce per-step AABB hem expansion or independently lagging inner fabric. Keep facing yaw on the actor root and local pose rotations on the body; verify crawl at cardinal/diagonal headings and crawl-to-stand seam normals.
