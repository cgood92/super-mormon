# Alma war adventures: source and staging notes

- **Morianton's Flight** follows [Alma 50:25–36](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/alma/50?lang=eng). The maidservant reports Morianton's plan; Moroni sends Teancum; Teancum intercepts the company at the narrow pass by the sea on both sides; Morianton dies in battle; the remaining people covenant to keep peace and return home. The exact coastline, boulders, six-soldier patrol, rope perimeter, placement guides, escape behavior, and containment challenge are invented for play. The player is Teancum; the six soldiers can be stationed and recalled, adjacent posts hold a rope, and the sixth closes it. A valid uncrossed perimeter must contain the entire company for four active seconds. The first placed soldier alerts the company to flee toward a visibly marked north exit, allowing time to inspect the clearing before starting. An escape resets only the challenge, preserving the warning. The rope victory is followed by a brief account of the scriptural battle and peace covenant; it does not change Morianton’s recorded fate. Costumes are imaginative staging; clothing and equipment distinguish the groups, never complexion. The maidservant's mistreatment is reported without a visual reenactment.
- **Teancum's Night Mission** follows [Alma 51:32–37](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/alma/51?lang=eng). Teancum and his servant go out at night, enter Amalickiah's sleeping camp, Teancum uses a javelin, and they return to wake and prepare their own men. The playable patrols and tent placement are imagined. The death occurs in a brief non-graphic cutaway. This is Amalickiah in Alma 51, not the later Ammoron mission in Alma 62.
  The expanded route, six watch lines, stone cover, western grove, canvas tent shapes, bedding, costumes and action wording are gameplay inventions or visual reconstructions. Scripture does not establish these tent designs or patrols. Watch objectives advance through movement; only meeting the servant, the bedside mission and alerting the home guard require an action. The king's death is conveyed through brief narration without a rendered strike or injury.
- **The Rescue at Gid** follows [Alma 55:4–24](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/alma/55?lang=eng). Laman, a descendant of Laman, takes wine to the guards; when they sleep he reports to Moroni; Moroni's men cast weapons over the walls to the prisoners, including women and children able to use them; Moroni surrounds the city, the captains surrender, and the prisoners are freed without bloodshed. The supply cache, two carried bundles, wall-kick entry, player dropping arms on the courtyard ground, automatic prisoner collection, rising timber gate, and armed procession before surrender are invented gameplay details. Scripture says weapons were cast in to the prisoners and the captains surrendered when they saw armed prisoners within and Nephites without; it does not describe Moroni jumping into Gid or a gate-opening procession. The terrain, architecture, costumes and brief dialogue are reconstructions, not established geography or quotations. Rope ladders belong to the separate Nephihah account in Alma 62:18–26, not this Gid account.

- **The Taking of Nephihah** follows [Alma 62:18–29](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/alma/62?lang=eng). Moroni scouts from the wall at night, sees the army asleep on the east by the entrance, returns to his army, and has cords and ladders prepared. His army enters on the west before morning. The defenders flee by the pass; the ensuing pursuit includes Lamanite deaths and prisoners, while Moroni and Pahoran recover the city without losing a Nephite soul. Willing prisoners later join the people of Ammon. The playable scene depicts scouting, the crossing and flight, without reenacting the deaths or adding a mandatory prisoner conversation.
  - The city plan, three projecting jumping stones, visible equipment, small representative army, wooden stair-like ladder treads, and pressure-based steering of defenders are gameplay interpretations. Scripture does not describe Moroni's climbing method or sheep-like wandering. No invented dialogue is presented as scripture.
  - Scouting, rallying the army, entering the city and completion advance through movement. The only required action deploys both ladders from near the western wall; placement uses a clear central lane so the inner descent cannot pass through dwellings. Followers use grounded route points and physical movement over the treads. The defenders respond to Moroni's approach, retain completed exits, and finish the adventure when all five representatives are outside the eastern pass.
