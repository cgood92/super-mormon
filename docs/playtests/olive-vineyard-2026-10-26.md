# The Olive Vineyard playtest revision

Primary source: [Jacob 5](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/jacob/5?lang=eng). Jacob quotes Zenos's allegory. The mother tree's decay and tender growth follow verses 3–6; wild grafts and scattering follow 7–14; selected distant plantings and mixed fruit follow 15–28; later corrupt fruit, the plea for more time and gathering back follow 29–60; servants, gradual removal, renewed good fruit and joy follow 61–77.

This symbolic garden condenses repeated visits and long periods into sixteen grouped objectives. Player role, unnamed servant appearance, layout, tools, soil colors, animations, dialogue and camera tours are invented teaching/game framing. Three plantings represent selected trees in the allegory, not a complete geography or a claim that every named people maps to one specific tree. No actor portrays the Lord. The ending shows the season of good fruit and joy rather than reenacting the final burning.

Meeting and consulting the servant happen by proximity. Gardening requires one fresh action press. Pruning and digging share an action; nourishing leaves visible root mulch and young branches. Wild and natural branches have visible source props. Three tender cuttings attach to the character's right hand socket and disappear individually as planted. Grafting and tree care use trunk proximity, independent of the beacon position. The inspection tour shows poor, poorer and choice plantings automatically; the ending tour includes the recovered mother tree and distant plantings. Repetitive asking, trimming and nourishing stops are condensed into the servant's account of the work.

Checks cover the full grouped story, single-press input, hand attachment and inventory, ordinary physical walking between actions, solid trunks, semantic scenery ownership, instance disposal, servant conversation framing on phone/desktop, and rendered-scene clearance of the automatic camera tours. Browser visual review remains with the integration agent.
